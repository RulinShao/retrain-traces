# Shared skills (new line: retrain_min and retrain_fixed)

Optional, agent-facing scripts and notes. Pass this directory with `--skills skills/shared` to either harness: it is
copied into the workspace as `skills/optional/` (apart from the mandatory tool docs in `skills/tools/` and
`skills/trainer/`) and the system prompt gains two lines (where they are; append lessons to playbook_suggestions.md).
`PLAYBOOK.md` (<= 2,500 characters, versioned) is also inlined into the system prompt under "Playbook
(skills/optional/PLAYBOOK.md: optional advice from earlier sessions; you may follow or ignore it)"; the session records the version
(RESULT.json `skills_version`, SCOREBOARD.md header). Nothing else changes; the agent may use, edit or ignore them.
A skill is a directory with a SKILL.md and, optionally, scripts that run in the workspace with no other service.

| skill | what it does |
|---|---|
| `reward_check/` | `check_reward.py REWARD [--results FILE]`: runs your reward(result) on example (or your own) sim replies and prints the scores |

The other directories under `skills/` (decompose, library, reward_check, snapshot, train_grpo, write_practice,
write_skill, gateway.py, README.md, ADAPTERS.md) are the frozen old loop's seed skills: they call `skills/gateway.py`,
which only `retrain/` serves, and they stay in place because `retrain/workspace.py` (SEED) and `envs/` job scripts read
them by path. See `../OLD_LOOP.md`.

# Playbook
version: 8 (2026-10-23; item 1 shaped restack transfer; v7 note)
Changelog policy: edited by humans from behaviour digests; agents may propose changes via playbook_suggestions.md.

Actions earlier sessions found worth exploring (suggestions, not rules):
1. Consider training where the base already succeeds sometimes. Runs starting at 0% success got no task_completed signal. Shaped reward never lifted the 0% task itself (6 of 6 runs: 0-2/100), but one 50-step shaped restack run raised lift 67->84, stack 51->64, wipe 16->35.
2. GRPO learns from reward differences within a rollout group: a group whose rollouts all get the same reward gives no gradient. Changing the start state or reward helped more than training longer.
3. An adapter whose scoreboard state reads "weights unchanged (all-zero update)" learned nothing; check that before building on it.
4. You can continue training from your best adapter instead of base; earlier records show no clear win either way.
5. Consider keeping several adapters: the score is the loaded stack over every task. One adapter trained on six tasks together averaged 6.7 points above per-task specialists; one on a composite task scored below base.
6. Evaluate a candidate on dev seeds of every task before loading it: one adapter loaded without that check lost 37 of 100 on another task.
7. Gains from more steps on one task came by about step 120 and were flat after.
8. Loading is free and the session can end at any time: load your best evaluated stack as soon as you have one. After 5 h, none of 8 earlier sessions had loaded anything.
9. An eval's cost is mostly its fixed start-up: 100 seeds cost little more than 20 and are far less noisy.
10. Jobs can queue for hours and wait.py blocks your turn: submit the jobs you already want together, then wait.
11. Hold out dev seeds you never train on (keep `seed_start` away from the seeds eval.py draws); before loading an adapter, compare its score on training seeds (rewards.json) with a held-out eval and prefer the held-out number.
12. Plan the budget up front: besides training, reserve enough for the evals and loads you will need. Committing most of it early to tasks with signal worked when those checks were planned in.

What did not work:
- A put-down practice task starting with the cube in hand: 1 of 640 rollouts succeeded, too little signal.
- A `note`/`goal` (training prompt only) did not transfer: one adapter completed 22% of training rollouts with a note, 0/100 at eval.
