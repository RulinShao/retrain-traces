## SFT (`objective: sft`)
A spec `{"objective": "sft", "data": "<file>.jsonl"}` (optional: `name`, `init`, `epochs` (1), `batch` (16 rows per step), `lr`
- Steps = ceil(kept rows x epochs / batch); rows train in a fixed shuffled order; a row longer than 4096 tokens is dropped (`call.json`
  `sft_facts.dropped_too_long`). At most 20000 rows, 256 MB, 65536 characters per message or program.
- Building the file: `python3 skills/trainer/sft_rows.py practice adapters/<name> [--min-reward 1] [--steps 3-5] > sft.jsonl` takes a
  finished practice call's rollouts (Output below); `python3 skills/trainer/sft_rows.py rollouts rollouts/<id>.jsonl [--min-reward 1] > sft.jsonl`
- Output: `adapters/<name>/` with the adapter, `sft_log.json` (per step: `step`, `loss`, `grad_norm`, `tokens`), `call.json` and
- Cost: 1 GPU x (0.05 + 0.000695 x steps x batch) canonical GPU-hours (the admission estimate counts every row of the file).
Optional. `{"plateau_steps": K, "min_gain": g}` stops the call once K steps in a row pass without the smoothed reward rising more
holds stops it. The smoothed reward is the mean `score_mean` of the last 3 steps, so neither can stop a call before step 3
- `reward check (before any spend): <file> scores its start state / a crashed program at or above the median of programs that ran (start-state <x> (n <k>), crash <y> (n <k>), running median <z> (n <k>)); ...`: a `.py` reward is scored on your own recorded rows of the task (rollouts/*.jsonl and adapters/*/practice_rows.jsonl: rows that never moved, rows that crashed, rows that ran) before the train starts; nothing is charged. `"allow_noop_reward": true` in the spec trains anyway (recorded); otherwise the numbers are in the train's result (`reward_check`).
- sft: `train: data line <N>: <rule>` (a row's keys, task, seed reserved, prompt, program or reward), `train: no row has reward >= min_reward <m> ...`, `train: field(s) [...] have no meaning with objective 'sft'; ...`, `train: objective 'sft' needs data: ...`, `train: objective 'sft' trains the small policy profiles on this site ...`, `train: steps <s> != ceil(kept rows x epochs / batch) = <n>; omit steps`, `train: task: an sft spec trains the tasks of its kept rows (...); omit task`
- `train: data has <rows> rows, not a multiple of batch <batch>: ...` / `train: steps <s> != data rows / batch = ...; omit steps` / `train: task: a spec with data trains the tasks its rows name (...); omit task` / `train: note cannot be set with data; ...`
canonical cost of the steps it completed, or less if it ran only briefly. The cost is also the admission estimate.
The job result of a finished train also carries `cost` (site facts, GPU-hours): `charged_this_call`, `charged_session`, `reserved_running` (the estimates of your calls still running), `available` (budget - charged - reserved), `budget`, `measured_this_call` (hardware time, not charged), `queue_s` / `run_s`, and `marginal_gpu_hours`: `one_more_step` of this call, `eval_first_seed` and `eval_per_additional_seed` of a dev eval. Quotes, not reserves: nothing is set aside for you. `charged_session_by_kind` is `charged_session` split by call kind: `train` (train calls, sft included), `eval` (dev evals and rollout.py calls) and `other` (every other charged call).
`rewards.json` (per step: `step`, `score_mean` over that step's rollouts), `call.json` (job ids, `gpu_hours` = the canonical charge to
your budget, `gpu_hours_actual` = measured hardware time, not charged, nonzero LoRA-B count, rollout seed range) and `training.json` (`resolved_spec`: the controls actually used). The adapter can then be named in `eval.py --adapter`, `load.py`, or as `init` of another train.
Each `rewards.json` step also has `zero_variance_frac` (the share of that step's prompt groups whose rollouts all got one reward) and `failure_stages` (failed rollouts by stage: `sim`, `timeout`, `program`, `grader`, `task`); the running job's status line shows them for the latest step and the train's result lists them per step (`step_facts`).
A call of several tasks (a `task` list, or `data` naming several) adds `per_task` to each `rewards.json` step: `{task: {"score_mean", "rollouts"}}`, that step's rollouts of each task and their mean reward (a rollout is attributed by its prompt; one matching no single task is left out).
Each finished train (and one kept at a stop) also writes `practice_rows.jsonl`: one line per rollout of the call, `{task, seed, prompt_sha, program, reward, step, start}` plus the episode's `stdout_tail`, `sandbox_rc`, `secs`, `task_completed`, `sim_reward` (the reply's `reward`), `stages`, `final_state` and `trace` (reward = the score GRPO gave it; start = the adapter the call started from), with the prompts in `practice_prompts.json` (`{prompt_sha: [chat messages]}`). A rollout whose program was written for two seeds in one step is left out. These are training data you generated yourself; `skills/trainer/sft_rows.py practice adapters/<name>` turns them into SFT rows (see SFT below).
Each such train also writes `train_data_digest.md` (and the same as `train_data_digest.json`) next to `practice_rows.jsonl`, over its rows: rollouts, unique programs, nonzero-reward share, GRPO groups (the rollouts of one prompt) with the all-zero and zero-variance ones; the same per step and, for a call of several tasks, per task; and the 3 highest- and 3 lowest-reward rollouts with task, step, seed, reward, program and their line in `practice_rows.jsonl`. The train's job result carries `train_data`: the digest's path and those counts.
The job result of a finished train also carries `receipt` (site facts): `curve` (per step: `score_mean`, `zero_variance_frac`, `rollouts_per_step`, and `per_task` curves for a multi-task call), `slopes` (mean change of `score_mean` per step over the last 3 steps and over the whole call; numbers over those windows, nothing more), `stop` (`state`, `reason` = the stop rule's reason, a cluster stop, or `max_steps`; `steps_run`, `steps_declared`, `adapter_step`), `lineage` (`parent` = `init`), `seeds` (the range and count the rollouts used), and `evals` (how many dev evals this adapter and its parent already have in this session; `marker: "not evaluated"` when it has none). It states facts; it does not rank adapters or say when to stop.
`score_center` (boolean, default false; this trainer only): true trains each step with score centering (Eq. 8 of arXiv 2609.20807). Sampling also returns the sampler's top 32 token log-probabilities at every sampled position; the update subtracts at every position the sampler's expected score over those 32 tokens, the remaining probability mass modelled by the trainer's distribution rescaled to the sampler's. When the trainer's and the sampler's next-token distributions agree it changes nothing; it differs from the default update when they disagree (a large mean or max |log p - log q| or KL between trainer and sampler).
