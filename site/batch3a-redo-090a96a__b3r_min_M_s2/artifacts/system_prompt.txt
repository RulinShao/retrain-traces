# Playbook
version: 5 (2026-10-22; 2: shaped reward on 0% tasks, hyak batch 3a; v4: 13; v3: 10-12, note/goal)
Changelog policy: edited by humans from behaviour digests; agents may propose changes via playbook_suggestions.md.

Actions earlier sessions found worth exploring (suggestions, not rules):
1. Probe cheaply before spending: a small dev-seed eval of base on each task costs little and shows where training can start.
2. Consider training where the base already succeeds sometimes. Earlier practice runs that started at 0% success got no signal; those starting at 5% or more improved. Shaped reward did not rescue 0% tasks: 5 of 5 such runs ended at 0-2/100.
3. GRPO learns from reward differences within a rollout group: a group whose rollouts all get the same reward gives no gradient. Changing the start state or reward helped more than training longer.
4. An adapter whose scoreboard state reads "weights unchanged (all-zero update)" learned nothing; check that before building on it.
5. You can continue training from your best adapter instead of base; earlier records show no clear win either way.
6. Consider keeping several adapters: the score is the loaded stack over every task. One adapter trained on six tasks together averaged 6.7 points above per-task specialists, but a joint adapter on a composite task scored below base.
7. Evaluate a candidate on dev seeds of every task before loading it: one adapter loaded without that check lost 37 of 100 on another task.
8. Gains from more steps on one task came by about step 120 and were flat after.
9. Keep an eye on remaining GPU-hours against the cost table; a single large train call can take most of the budget.
10. skills/trainer/SKILL.md lists every spec field; earlier sessions spent 16-77 turns reading code to find them.
11. Loading is free and the session can end at any time: load your best evaluated stack as soon as you have one. After 5 h, none of 8 earlier sessions had loaded anything.
12. An eval's cost is mostly its fixed start-up: 100 seeds cost little more than 20 and are far less noisy.
13. Jobs can queue for hours and wait.py blocks your turn: submit the jobs you already want together, then wait.

What did not work:
- A put-down practice task starting with the cube in hand: 1 of 640 rollouts succeeded, too little signal.
- Practice prompts that differed from the evaluation prompt (`note`, `goal`) were the leading suspected cause of weak trained adapters.
